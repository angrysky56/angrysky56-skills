# ASEKE Example Applications

This reference provides real-world analytical walkthroughs demonstrating the 5-step Analytical Query Framework in action across various workplace, community, and organizational scenarios.

---

## Example 1: "Why does my coworker resist every change?"

### Step 1 (System Identification)
- **Primary systems**: FEAR (change = threat to known environment) + SEEKING frustrated (can't predict outcomes).
- **Timescale**: Chronic pattern sustained by organizational environment.
- **CE load**: Moderate — they can still reason, but their reasoning is FEAR-colored.

### Step 2 (IS Capture Analysis)
- **Dominant IS**: "The current system works; change introduces risk."
- **IS Source**: Lived experience of poorly-implemented changes causing real harm. This IS is experientially grounded — not irrational.

### Step 3 (Trajectory Assessment)
- **Trajectory**: Stable loop — each new change confirms the FEAR substrate, which strengthens the IS, which increases resistance.

### Step 4 (Blind Spot Identification)
- **Subject's blind spot**: Subject can't see that the current system ALSO has risks (FEAR makes the known feel safe regardless of its actual safety).
- **Analyst's blind spot**: Analyst (you) may have SEEKING-dominant activation making all change feel exciting — your PLAY/SEEKING substrate makes their FEAR look like obstruction.

### Step 5 (Strategy Generation)
- Don't argue change is good (this triggers FEAR defense).
- Address the system: "What specifically are you worried will break?" treats their FEAR as useful threat-detection intelligence rather than obstruction.
- Make the change SEEKING-compatible by showing you've accounted for their concerns.
- Activate their SEEKING toward the change rather than suppressing their FEAR about it.

## Example 2: "Why is this online community radicalizing?"

### Step 1 (System Identification)
- **Primary systems**: SEEKING (explanation-hungry) → FEAR (threat content activates threat-detection) → RAGE (FEAR blocked from escape converts to fight).
- **Timescale**: Chronic escalation.
- **CE load**: CE demand increases at each stage, reducing capacity to evaluate the IS driving the escalation.

### Step 2 (IS Capture Analysis)
- Multiple IS competed; the winner provides the simplest SEEKING satisfaction (an explanation) + clearest RAGE target + strongest PANIC/GRIEF relief (community belonging).
- Algorithmically amplified (Pattern 5: Algorithmic Escalation Ladder) and socially reinforced via contagion.

### Step 3 (Trajectory Assessment)
- Positive feedback loop. Community dynamics push FEAR/RAGE higher → higher CE demand → less capacity to evaluate IS → more extreme IS adoptable → higher FEAR/RAGE.
- Each step up the ladder makes the next step easier and reversal harder.

### Step 4 (Blind Spot Identification)
- **Subject's blind spot**: Members can't see the loop because they're inside it — their SEEKING satisfaction ("I finally understand the world") is real, just not truth-tracking.
- **Analyst's blind spot**: Outside analysts may dismiss genuine underlying FEAR (which is often about real things: economic precarity, social change, loss of status) because the RAGE expression is extreme.

### Step 5 (Strategy Generation)
- Intervening on the IS directly (debunking) triggers RAGE defense + Identity Lock-In (Pattern 12).
- Intervene on the SUBSTRATE: address genuine FEAR sources, provide alternative community that meets PANIC/GRIEF needs (belonging) at lower FEAR/RAGE activation.
- Redirect SEEKING toward investigation that doesn't require RAGE discharge. This is harder and slower than debunking but it works.

## Example 3: "Two departments in our company are in constant conflict"

### Step 1 (System Identification)
- Mirror Conflict (Pattern 6). Both departments in FEAR (the other department threatens our resources/autonomy) + RAGE (defending our territory).
- CARE narrowed to own department.
- PLAY suppressed between departments (no cross-team humor, no collaborative exploration).

### Step 2 (IS Capture Analysis)
- Each department has an IS that frames the other as the problem.
- **IS source**: Genuine past experiences of the other department causing real harm (missed deadlines, resource competition, conflicting priorities). Both IS are experientially grounded.

### Step 3 (Trajectory Assessment)
- Self-sustaining dual attractor. Each side's defensive RAGE confirms the other's FEAR narrative.
- Without intervention, escalation or cold-war stalemate.

### Step 4 (Blind Spot Identification)
- Both sides see their behavior as defensive and the other's as aggressive.
- Leadership may see "personality conflicts" rather than structural IS capture. The structural driver (resource competition, conflicting incentives) is invisible to anyone focused on interpersonal dynamics.

### Step 5 (Strategy Generation)
- Make the mirror visible — not by blaming either side but by naming the structural driver.
- Change the incentive structure so departments' SEEKING aims at shared targets.
- Create PLAY opportunities (cross-team activities that aren't forced fun but genuine collaborative problem-solving).
- Activate CARE across the boundary through shared-vulnerability exposure ("we're all dealing with the same budget pressure").
