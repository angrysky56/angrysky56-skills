## ASEKE Analysis System Core Framework

### The Three Questions
For any behavioral pattern, ask:
1. **SUBSTRATE**: What primary emotional system(s) are active, and at what intensity?
2. **CAPTURE**: What Information Structure has converted that emotional activation into this specific commitment/action?
3. **TRAJECTORY**: Where is this state heading — what transitions are likely, and what would trigger them?

### The Two Timescales
- **ACUTE** (minutes to hours): Momentary emotional states that bias judgment RIGHT NOW. Relevant for negotiations, arguments, crisis response.
- **CHRONIC** (months to years): Dispositional orientations shaped by sustained emotional environments. Relevant for understanding worldviews, institutional culture, political identity.

## The Biological Substrate: Seven Primary Emotional Systems

The biological foundation uses Panksepp's Affective Neuroscience framework (1998) — seven primary emotional systems identified through electrical brain stimulation, pharmacological challenges, and lesion studies across mammalian species. These are not metaphors. They are distinct neural circuits with specific neuroanatomy and neurochemistry, homologously conserved across mammals.

**Why Panksepp over the circumplex**: Russell's circumplex model (valence × arousal) describes the *space* emotions occupy but can't distinguish states that occupy the same coordinates. Anger and fear are both high-arousal/negative-valence — the circumplex can't tell them apart despite their being completely different neural systems with different behavioral outputs, different neurochemistry, and different IS vulnerabilities. Panksepp's systems give us the resolution the circumplex lacks.

### SEEKING (Expectancy/Desire)
**Neural basis**: Mesolimbic dopamine pathway (VTA → nucleus accumbens → prefrontal cortex). The most foundational system — it energizes all goal-directed behavior.
**Functional role**: Drives exploration, curiosity, anticipation, appetitive motivation. The "engine" that gets organisms to engage with the world. Not pleasure itself — the *wanting* that precedes pleasure.
**Behavioral signature**: Forward-leaning engagement, novelty pursuit, energized investigation, optimistic planning. "I want to find out" and "I want to get that."
**CE demand**: Moderate. SEEKING is energizing but not overwhelming — it enhances cognitive flexibility rather than consuming attentional resources.
**IS vulnerability**: SEEKING is the system IS competes for most intensely. When SEEKING is active, the organism is *looking for explanations and goals*. An IS that provides a compelling narrative target for SEEKING energy captures the person's motivational engine. This is why radicalization often begins with genuine curiosity — "I just wanted to understand" — before the SEEKING energy gets channeled by a specific IS. Cults, ideological movements, and conspiracy theories all hook SEEKING first.
**Chronic suppression**: When SEEKING is chronically under-activated (anhedonia, depression), the organism stops engaging with the world entirely. This is the burnout cascade's endpoint — not just tiredness but the extinction of wanting itself.

### RAGE (Anger)
**Neural basis**: Medial amygdala → hypothalamus → PAG (periaqueductal gray). Triggered by frustration of SEEKING (goal blockage), restraint, or territorial violation.
**Functional role**: Overcome obstacles, defend resources, signal dominance. An approach emotion — RAGE moves *toward* the threat, unlike FEAR which moves away.
**Behavioral signature**: Aggressive posture, confrontational language, boundary assertion, dominance displays. Heightened energy directed at a specific target. "Get out of my way" and "This is mine."
**CE demand**: High. RAGE narrows attention to the target and suppresses deliberative processing. This is functional in actual combat but catastrophic for complex decision-making. The RAGE-captured person cannot simultaneously analyze whether their target is correctly identified.
**IS vulnerability**: RAGE requires a *target*. When RAGE is active but the target is ambiguous, any IS that provides one gets adopted with relief (Pattern 1: Scapegoat Pivot). RAGE is the emotional substrate most easily redirected by demagogic IS — the anger is real but the aim is manufactured.
**Distinction from FEAR**: Both are high-arousal/negative-valence, but neurologically and behaviorally opposite. RAGE approaches; FEAR withdraws. RAGE narrows on a target; FEAR scans for escape. Misidentifying which system is active leads to completely wrong interventions — de-escalating FEAR requires safety signals; de-escalating RAGE requires removing the perceived obstacle or threat to resources.

### FEAR (Anxiety)
**Neural basis**: Central/lateral amygdala → hypothalamus → PAG. Distinct from RAGE circuit despite amygdala involvement — different nuclei, different outputs.
**Functional role**: Detect and escape danger. Freeze when escape is impossible. The system that keeps organisms alive by making them attend to threats with absolute priority.
**Behavioral signature**: Vigilance, scanning, startle response, avoidance, flight. At extreme activation: freeze/immobility. "Something is wrong" and "I need to get out."
**CE demand**: Very high. FEAR commandeers attentional resources for threat monitoring, leaving minimal capacity for anything else. This is the "weapon focus effect" — under FEAR, attention narrows to the threat source and everything else disappears. The person literally cannot think about other things, not because they won't but because the architecture won't let them.
**IS vulnerability**: FEAR is the primary substrate for authoritarian IS capture. Chronic FEAR activation primes dangerous-world beliefs (the RWA substrate per Duckitt, 2001). IS that promise *safety through order, strong leadership, and group cohesion* resonate directly with the FEAR system's functional logic: the system is designed to find safety, so any IS that offers a clear path to safety captures it. This is why authoritarian movements surge during periods of genuine or manufactured threat.
**Chronic activation**: Sustained FEAR produces anxiety disorders, hypervigilance, and the dangerous-world worldview that undergirds authoritarianism — not as ideology but as the lived experience of a nervous system stuck in threat-detection mode. The person doesn't *choose* to see the world as dangerous; their FEAR system is telling them it IS dangerous, continuously.
**Distinction from RAGE**: FEAR wants to *escape*; RAGE wants to *attack*. When FEAR is blocked from escape (no exit available), it can convert to RAGE — the cornered animal that fights. This FEAR→RAGE conversion is a critical transition: it changes the behavioral output from flight to fight while the underlying distress remains the same. IS that offer targets for RAGE provide a discharge path for trapped FEAR.

### PANIC/GRIEF (Separation Distress)
**Neural basis**: Anterior cingulate → bed nucleus of stria terminalis → PAG. Opioid and oxytocin systems critically involved — social connection is literally analgesic; social loss is literally painful.
**Functional role**: Maintain social bonds. Signal distress when separated from attachment figures or group. The system that makes mammals *need* each other — not as preference but as biological imperative.
**Behavioral signature**: Crying, calling out, clinging, searching for the lost other. Withdrawal, loss of motivation when the bond is permanently broken. "Where are you?" becomes "Nothing matters."
**CE demand**: Moderate-to-high. Active grief consumes attentional resources through rumination and search behaviors. Resolved grief (processing complete) has low CE demand. Unresolved grief cycles between high and low.
**IS vulnerability**: PANIC/GRIEF makes people desperately seek belonging. Any IS that offers a *community* — a replacement for the lost connection — captures this system powerfully. This is why bereavement, divorce, job loss, and social exclusion are prime recruitment windows for cults, extremist groups, and populist movements. The IS doesn't need to be accurate; it needs to offer belonging. "You're one of us" is the most powerful sentence in any language when PANIC/GRIEF is active.
**Connection to depression**: When PANIC/GRIEF becomes chronic without resolution, it produces the characteristic pattern of depression: SEEKING shuts down (anhedonia), social withdrawal increases, and the organism enters conservation mode. Panksepp argued depression is fundamentally a disorder of the PANIC/GRIEF system, not a "chemical imbalance" in the abstract.

### CARE (Nurturing)
**Neural basis**: Preoptic area of hypothalamus → VTA → PAG. Oxytocin and prolactin systems. Originally mapped through maternal behavior but extends to all nurturing bonds.
**Functional role**: Protect and nurture offspring and allies. The system that produces empathy, compassion, and protective behavior toward vulnerable others. Evolutionarily: ensure offspring survival. Socially: the substrate for all caretaking behavior.
**Behavioral signature**: Protective approach toward the vulnerable, attentive listening, physical comfort provision, self-sacrifice for others' wellbeing. "I will keep you safe" and "Let me help."
**CE demand**: Low-to-moderate. CARE is sustainable — it's designed for the long haul of child-rearing, not the sprint of threat response. This makes CARE-based political engagement more durable than RAGE-based or FEAR-based engagement.
**IS vulnerability**: CARE can be captured by IS that define who "deserves" care — who counts as "one of ours" versus "not our problem." Narrowing the circle of care is the mechanism of most in-group/out-group IS. Broadening it is the mechanism of universalist ethics. The CARE system is the biological substrate of the entire egalitarian-to-stratified axis: SDO is fundamentally a measure of how wide or narrow the circle of care extends.
**Political bridge**: CARE is the most direct biological substrate for egalitarian politics *when the circle extends broadly*, and for in-group protectionism *when the circle is narrow*. The system itself doesn't determine politics — the IS determines who falls inside the circle.

### PLAY (Social Joy)
**Neural basis**: Thalamic and hypothalamic circuits → dorsomedial diencephalon. Distinct from SEEKING — PLAY requires social partners; SEEKING can be solitary. Opioid involvement in bonding during play.
**Functional role**: Build social competence, test boundaries, establish trust. PLAY is how mammals learn the rules of social interaction without lethal consequences. Mock-fighting teaches real limits.
**Behavioral signature**: Laughter, rough-and-tumble, teasing, improvisation, spontaneous rule-making and rule-testing. "Let's see what happens" and "That was fun."
**CE demand**: Low. PLAY is the lowest-CE social state — it's intrinsically rewarding and doesn't require effortful processing. This is why humor is such an effective communication tool: it activates PLAY, which lowers defensive processing and opens cognitive flexibility.
**IS vulnerability**: PLAY is relatively resistant to IS capture because it operates below the narrative level — it's pre-verbal, body-based, and immediate. This makes PLAY a powerful de-escalation tool: you can't simultaneously be in PLAY and in RAGE or FEAR (Panksepp's research shows these systems inhibit each other). However, PLAY can be *weaponized* — mockery, ridicule, and trolling use PLAY's social dynamics to inflict PANIC/GRIEF (exclusion) while maintaining plausible deniability.
**Political bridge**: PLAY is the substrate of genuinely open engagement — curiosity without threat, competition without domination. Communities with high PLAY activation tend toward lower RWA and lower SDO because the world feels safe enough to play in and the hierarchy feels flexible enough to test. Suppression of PLAY (authoritarian environments that forbid spontaneity) is a reliable indicator of high-RWA institutional mood.

### LUST (Sexual/Appetitive Drive)
**Neural basis**: Hypothalamic circuits, distinct from SEEKING though they interact. Testosterone, estrogen, vasopressin, oxytocin involvement.
**Functional role**: Reproductive motivation. Included for completeness but less directly relevant to the analytical engine's political-behavioral focus.
**IS vulnerability**: LUST is exploited by advertising, political scandal narratives, and purity-based IS. The intersection of LUST and FEAR produces purity politics — the control of sexuality as proxy for social order. Relevant when analyzing moral panic patterns around sexual behavior.

### System Interactions: The Key to Behavioral Complexity

These seven systems don't operate in isolation. Behavior emerges from their *interaction*:

- **SEEKING + FEAR** = vigilant exploration (cautious investigation of threats). This combination produces the "concerned citizen" who is genuinely curious but also anxious — highly susceptible to IS that offer both explanation (satisfies SEEKING) and safety (satisfies FEAR).
- **RAGE + FEAR** = the cornered animal. When FEAR can't find escape, it converts to RAGE. This is the mechanism behind violence born of desperation — not cold aggression but terrified fury. IS that exploit this offer both a target for RAGE and a promise of safety from FEAR.
- **SEEKING + PLAY** = flow/creativity. The optimal state for learning and innovation. Low CE demand, high cognitive flexibility. This is the state that authoritarian environments systematically suppress because it produces unpredictable behavior.
- **CARE + PANIC/GRIEF** = empathic distress. Witnessing others' suffering activates CARE (wanting to help) and PANIC/GRIEF (resonating with their pain). This is the substrate of compassion fatigue — CARE is sustainable but PANIC/GRIEF is depleting.
- **SEEKING suppressed + PANIC/GRIEF chronic** = depression. The defining feature of clinical depression in Panksepp's framework: the wanting system goes offline while the loss system stays active. This is also the endpoint of the burnout cascade (Pattern 4).

### Critical Insights for the Analytical Engine

**Insight 1: IS captures systems, not "moods."** When we say "IS capture," we mean a narrative framework latches onto a specific primary emotional system's output. FEAR-capture produces authoritarian IS. RAGE-capture produces eliminationist IS. SEEKING-capture produces ideological commitment. PANIC/GRIEF-capture produces cult membership. CARE-capture (narrowed) produces in-group protectionism. Each system has a different IS vulnerability profile.

**Insight 2: Valence direction is IS-determined.** The raw activation of FEAR or RAGE doesn't have political content until an IS provides a target. The same FEAR system that produces progressive environmentalism ("the planet is in danger") also produces right-wing nationalism ("our culture is in danger"). The substrate is identical; the IS aim is different.

**Insight 3: Positive valence is not neutral.** Per System Justification Theory (Jost & Banaji, 1994), when SEEKING is satisfied and FEAR is quiet, the organism actively rationalizes the system that produced this comfort. SJT shows this operates even for the disadvantaged — justifying the system has a "palliative function" that reduces the anxiety acknowledging injustice would produce.

**Insight 4: CE demand varies by system, not just by arousal.** FEAR and RAGE are both high-arousal but consume CE differently: FEAR broadens attention (scanning for escape routes) while RAGE narrows it (targeting). PLAY is moderate-arousal but very low CE demand. PANIC/GRIEF is low-arousal but high CE demand (rumination). This is why the old arousal = CE demand mapping was too simple.

**Insight 5: System transitions change behavioral output completely.** The same person under FEAR (avoidant, scanning, anxious) behaves as a fundamentally different agent than under RAGE (confrontational, targeted, certain). The IS that fits each state is different. The intervention that works for each is different. Misidentifying which system is active is the most common analytical error.

## The Bridge: Emotional Systems → Political Orientation

The Dual Process Model (Duckitt & Sibley, 2009) identifies two independent dimensions of political orientation: RWA (dangerous-world beliefs / security need) and SDO (competitive-world beliefs / hierarchy acceptance). The bridge to Panksepp:

**FEAR → RWA**: Chronic FEAR activation produces the dangerous-world perception that undergirds RWA. This is not a metaphor — sustained threat-detection literally produces the worldview that the world is a dangerous place requiring strong authority and in-group cohesion. The person isn't "choosing" authoritarianism; their nervous system is reporting danger and they're responding accordingly.

**RAGE → SDO (when aimed down hierarchy)**: RAGE at out-groups or subordinates reinforces dominance hierarchies. RAGE at elites or hierarchy itself undermines them. Same system, different IS aim, opposite political output.

**PANIC/GRIEF → belongingness-seeking**: The desperate need for connection produced by PANIC/GRIEF activation makes people susceptible to any IS that offers community membership. This is politically agnostic — it can produce left solidarity or right tribalism depending on which community is available.

**CARE (broad) → low SDO**: When CARE extends broadly (universalist concern), it directly opposes hierarchy acceptance. When CARE narrows to in-group only, it reinforces hierarchy by defining who matters.

**SEEKING → IS acquisition**: SEEKING is the system through which ALL IS capture operates. It's the gateway. Active SEEKING + available IS = capture. Suppressed SEEKING = no capture but also no engagement (depression/apathy).

**PLAY → low RWA, low SDO**: Active PLAY requires a world experienced as safe enough to play in and a hierarchy flexible enough to test. PLAY activation is inherently anti-authoritarian and anti-hierarchical — not as ideology but as functional incompatibility.

**The timescale caveat**: These bridges operate at different speeds. FEAR→RWA is fast acutely (a single threat can shift attitudes temporarily) and slow chronically (sustained threat exposure reshapes worldview over months). CARE→SDO is almost entirely chronic — empathy breadth changes through sustained relationship exposure, not through argument. This is why the two-timescale distinction matters: acute interventions target system activation; chronic interventions target dispositional patterns.

## PATTERN LIBRARY

### Pattern 1: THE SCAPEGOAT PIVOT
**Substrate**: RAGE active, target ambiguous. Often FEAR→RAGE conversion (cornered animal).
**What's happening**: RAGE requires a target. When RAGE is active but the source of frustration is diffuse or structural (and therefore hard to attack), any IS that provides a concrete target gets adopted with relief. The emotional substrate (genuine distress) is real; the target assignment is IS-determined.
**Recognize it by**: Sudden certainty about who's to blame after a period of diffuse anxiety. Relief mixed with rage — the relief comes from RAGE finally having somewhere to go.
**Acute version**: A frustrated employee suddenly "realizes" a coworker is the problem.
**Chronic version**: An economically stressed population "discovers" that immigrants are the cause.
**Blind spot of the subject**: The relief of target acquisition feels like insight. "I finally see clearly" is the phenomenology of SEEKING satisfaction (explanation found), not truth.
**Blind spot of the analyst**: Dismissing the underlying distress because the target is wrong. The RAGE substrate is real even when the IS aim is manufactured.
**Leverage points**: Address the substrate (the SEEKING frustration and FEAR underneath the RAGE) rather than arguing about the target. Arguing the target strengthens the IS by activating RAGE defense.

### Pattern 2: THE COHERENCE TIMEOUT
**Substrate**: Any high-CE-demand system (FEAR, RAGE) sustained at tempo that exceeds deliberative capacity.
**What's happening**: The ethical/analytical "boundary condition" fails because FEAR or RAGE consumes all available attentional resources (per cognitive load theory, Sweller 1988). Not a character failure — an architectural one. System 2 requires attentional resources; the primary emotional system has commandeered them.
**Recognize it by**: Increasing reliance on heuristics, checklists, and authority deference. The form of oversight persists while its substance evaporates. "I reviewed it" means "I glanced at it."
**Acute version**: An analyst rubber-stamping at 86-second intervals in a targeting cycle.
**Chronic version**: An organization where "compliance" becomes performative — all boxes checked, no genuine assessment occurring.
**Blind spot of the subject**: They genuinely believe they're doing due diligence. The procedural form provides cognitive cover.
**Blind spot of the analyst**: Attributing the failure to individual laziness or corruption rather than tempo-capacity mismatch.
**Leverage points**: Reduce tempo. Mandatory deliberation periods. Structural impossibility of processing faster than assessment capacity. AI should extend the coherence window, not compress it.

### Pattern 3: THE COMFORT TRAP (System Justification)
**Substrate**: SEEKING satisfied, FEAR quiet, CARE narrowed to immediate circle. The system is at rest.
**What's happening**: Per System Justification Theory (Jost & Banaji, 1994), when primary emotional needs are met, the organism actively rationalizes the system that produced this comfort. This is not passive — it's motivated cognition. SJT shows the disadvantaged also justify systems that harm them, because acknowledging injustice would activate FEAR and PANIC/GRIEF, which system justification suppresses.
**Recognize it by**: "I'm not political." "Why can't people just work hard?" Genuine puzzlement at others' grievances. Also: disadvantaged people defending systems that exploit them.
**Blind spot of the subject**: System justification IS is the most invisible of all IS because it doesn't feel like a belief — it feels like the absence of belief. "The system is fair" is experienced as neutral observation, not ideology.
**Blind spot of the analyst**: Assuming bad faith where there's genuine motivated cognition. Also: SJT's most disturbing finding is that it operates across the hierarchy.
**Leverage points**: Experiences that activate CARE broadly without triggering FEAR. Narrative empathy (activates CARE + PANIC/GRIEF resonance via stories, not statistics). Address the FEAR that system justification suppresses — people justify the system because the alternative is frightening.

### Pattern 4: THE BURNOUT CASCADE
**Substrate**: RAGE → RAGE (controlled) → PANIC/GRIEF → SEEKING extinction. Each stage is a system transition, not a mood change.
**What's happening**: Sustained RAGE without resolution depletes the system. When RAGE energy exhausts, PANIC/GRIEF emerges (the loss of the fight). When PANIC/GRIEF goes unresolved, SEEKING shuts down (anhedonia). Each stage looks like a different "kind of person" but is the same person at different depletion levels.
**Recognize it by**: Stage 1 (RAGE): passionate advocacy, absolutist language. Stage 2 (controlled RAGE): cynical engagement. Stage 3 (PANIC/GRIEF): "I just can't anymore." Stage 4 (SEEKING extinction): flat affect, "nothing matters."
**Blind spot of the subject**: At each stage, the current state feels like the "real" them.
**Blind spot of the analyst**: Treating each stage as a stable identity rather than a position on a depletion trajectory. Prescribing "engagement" to someone in stage 3-4 is like telling a depleted battery to try harder.
**Leverage points**: Stage-appropriate intervention. Stage 1-2: channel RAGE into sustainable structure. Stage 3: activate CARE (from others) to address PANIC/GRIEF. Stage 4: reactivate SEEKING through small, achievable goals — the dopaminergic system needs tiny wins to restart.

### Pattern 5: THE ALGORITHMIC ESCALATION LADDER
**Substrate**: SEEKING → FEAR → RAGE, driven by external content rather than lived experience.
**What's happening**: Engagement-optimized platforms exploit SEEKING (curiosity) to deliver FEAR-activating content, which escalates to RAGE when escape feels impossible. Each step feels like "waking up to reality" but is actually IS capture via sequential system activation.
**Recognize it by**: Political opinions that track content consumption rather than lived experience. Increasingly certain views about threats never personally encountered. "I did my own research."
**Blind spot of the subject**: SEEKING satisfaction (finding explanations) feels like education. FEAR activation feels like caring. RAGE certainty feels like clarity.
**Blind spot of the analyst**: Dismissing the person as stupid. The mechanism exploits universal mammalian neurochemistry.
**Leverage points**: Arousal awareness training. "Does this track my lived experience or my media consumption?" SEEKING can be redirected to other targets — the system just wants to explore, it doesn't care what.

### Pattern 6: THE MIRROR CONFLICT
**Substrate**: Both sides in FEAR + RAGE, each triggered by the other's RAGE behavior.
**What's happening**: Symmetrical FEAR→RAGE conversion. Each side's defensive RAGE confirms the other's FEAR narrative. Both have genuine FEAR substrates. Both have IS that identifies the OTHER as the threat.
**Recognize it by**: Symmetrical dehumanization. Identical rhetorical structures with different nouns. Both claiming victimhood.
**Blind spot of the subject**: Their RAGE looks like defense; the other's looks like aggression. This is architectural — the IS frames your actions as responses and theirs as provocations.
**Blind spot of the analyst**: Assuming one side must be "right." **The asymmetry test**: are both sides equally capable of harm? If one has structural power the other lacks, this is not a true mirror — it's an asymmetric conflict wearing mirror aesthetics.
**Leverage points**: Make the mirror visible. De-escalation requires SIMULTANEOUS FEAR reduction on both sides — unilateral de-escalation reads as weakness and invites exploitation. Activate PLAY if possible (shared humor is the fastest mirror-breaker because PLAY and RAGE inhibit each other).

### Pattern 7: THE IS COMPETITION
**Substrate**: Any active primary system, especially SEEKING + FEAR/RAGE/PANIC-GRIEF combinations.
**What's happening**: The emotional substrate exists (the pain is real). Multiple IS frameworks are competing to capture the active SEEKING energy — to EXPLAIN the activated FEAR/RAGE/GRIEF and DIRECT the response. The IS that wins determines the political outcome.
**Recognize it by**: A person or community in genuine distress receiving competing narratives. "Your suffering is because of immigrants / corporations / government / systemic racism / personal failure."
**Blind spot of the subject**: The winning IS feels like "finally understanding" — SEEKING satisfaction. The relief is real; it's just not truth-tracking.
**Blind spot of the analyst**: Assuming the "correct" IS should win on merits. IS competition is a CE-efficiency contest. The IS that requires less attentional resource to adopt wins, regardless of accuracy.
**Leverage points**: Pre-position IS before crisis (people adopt the framework they already have when FEAR activates). Reduce CE demand of accurate frameworks. Deliver through CARE-activated trusted sources, not abstract argument.

### Pattern 8: THE INSTITUTIONAL MOOD
**Substrate**: Chronic activation pattern sustained by organizational structure — tempo drives FEAR, incentives drive SEEKING toward specific targets, social norms suppress PLAY.
**What's happening**: Institutions create chronic emotional environments through tempo, incentive structures, social norms, and information flows. Members' primary system activation patterns shift to match the institutional mood regardless of prior dispositions.
**Recognize it by**: "I went in with strong values and got flipped within two years" (Dennis Gioia, Ford). Ethical fading: the ethical dimension gradually disappears from awareness (Tenbrunsel & Messick, 2004).
**Blind spot of the subject**: "This is just how things work here." The institutional mood becomes invisible through habituation.
**Blind spot of the analyst**: Attributing institutional behavior to individual bad actors rather than affective architecture. Replacing the CEO doesn't change the system activation pattern if the structural drivers remain.
**Leverage points**: Change the structural drivers. Tempo limits (reduce chronic FEAR). Incentive restructuring (redirect SEEKING). Create space for PLAY (the anti-authoritarian system). External oversight bodies operating at different tempo and in different affective environment.

### Pattern 9: THE VIRTUE FORTRESS
**Substrate**: CARE (self-directed) + RAGE (defensive) activated by identity threat. The person's CARE system is directed at protecting their moral self-image.
**What's happening**: Positive self-concept functions as an IS that prevents genuine self-examination. Criticism activates FEAR (identity threat) → RAGE (defense of self). The CARE system, instead of extending outward, turns inward to protect the self's moral status.
**Recognize it by**: "We could never do X" as identity statement. Disproportionate emotional reactions to accusations. Treating criticism as attack.
**Blind spot of the subject**: The moral identity IS is experienced as self, not as belief. Challenging it triggers FEAR, not reflection.
**Blind spot of the analyst**: Assuming stated values are either sincere or cynical. Usually both simultaneously — genuinely held AND functioning to prevent self-examination.
**Leverage points**: "Assuming your values are genuine, would you want to know if your behavior contradicts them?" activates CARE (for the values themselves) rather than RAGE (defense of identity). Structural accountability catches the gap regardless of self-concept.

### Pattern 10: THE AUTHORITY TRANSFER (Milgram)
**Substrate**: FEAR (situational) + SEEKING (for guidance) → agentic state. The SEEKING system, activated by uncertainty, finds an authority figure who provides direction. FEAR of the authority reinforces compliance. The individual's own CARE system (which would normally resist causing harm) gets overridden.
**What's happening**: Authority provides a pre-packaged IS that reduces CE demand to near zero. "I was told to" requires no deliberation. The person shifts from autonomous mode to "agentic state" — they experience themselves as an instrument of the authority. The ethical boundary condition transfers to the authority figure.
**Recognize it by**: "I was just following orders/protocol/the process." Moral distress — the person FEELS wrong (CARE system active) but acts anyway (authority overrides CARE).
**Blind spot of the subject**: "It's not my decision" feels like factual description rather than moral choice.
**Blind spot of the analyst**: Assuming the person lacks moral awareness. Milgram's subjects showed extreme distress. CARE was active; it was overridden, not absent.
**Leverage points**: "You seem uncomfortable — what is your own assessment?" reactivates autonomous CARE. Structural: establish Tier 1 non-negotiables that cannot be delegated to authority.

### Pattern 11: THE NARRATIVE GRAVITY WELL
**Substrate**: SEEKING active + CARE/FEAR/PANIC-GRIEF engaged by story characters. Stories activate multiple primary systems simultaneously through identification.
**What's happening**: Human neural architecture processes narratives by activating primary emotional systems vicariously — FEAR for threatened characters, CARE for vulnerable ones, SEEKING for the resolution. This multi-system activation is why stories require less CE than statistical reasoning: they leverage existing emotional infrastructure rather than requiring abstract processing.
**Recognize it by**: "I know someone who..." outweighing epidemiological data. Conspiracy theories (narratives with characters and plots) outcompeting structural analysis (abstract and statistical).
**Blind spot of the subject**: The story FEELS more real because it activates more primary systems. A single vivid case activates CARE, FEAR, SEEKING simultaneously; a thousand data points activate none.
**Blind spot of the analyst**: Assuming better data will fix the problem. The problem isn't information deficit — it's system activation architecture.
**Leverage points**: Embed accurate information IN narratives. Counter stories with stories, not statistics. Match the IS delivery format to the target systems.

### Pattern 12: THE IDENTITY LOCK-IN
**Substrate**: IS fused with self-concept → any challenge activates FEAR (identity threat) → RAGE (defense) → SEEKING suppressed (evidence can't update beliefs because the update pathway is blocked by threat-response).
**What's happening**: Identity-protective cognition (Kahan, 2017). Evidence contra identity-fused IS is processed through FEAR circuitry rather than SEEKING circuitry. You are not in a debate; you are in a threat encounter.
**Recognize it by**: Evidence paradoxically strengthening the belief. "If I'm wrong about this, what else am I wrong about?" Social networks built entirely around shared IS. People who'd rather lose relationships with outsiders than question the IS.
**Blind spot of the subject**: They don't experience themselves as defending a belief — they experience themselves as defending who they ARE.
**Blind spot of the analyst**: Presenting evidence as if it will help. At identity-fusion levels, evidence activates FEAR, not SEEKING.
**Leverage points**: Don't challenge the IS directly. "What would you need to see to change your mind?" is diagnostic: if the answer is "nothing," the IS is identity-fused and evidential approaches will fail. Find the values BEHIND the IS and activate CARE for those values. Provide off-ramps that preserve dignity and community.

## ANALYTICAL QUERY FRAMEWORK

When presented with a behavioral pattern, situation, or conflict to analyze:

### Step 1: SYSTEM IDENTIFICATION
**Question**: Which primary emotional system(s) are driving the observed behavior?
- **SEEKING**: Is this person/group actively searching for explanations, goals, or targets? (Forward-leaning, curious, motivated)
- **RAGE**: Is there targeted aggression, boundary enforcement, or obstacle-removal behavior? (Confrontational, certain, narrowed attention)
- **FEAR**: Is there scanning, avoidance, vigilance, or escape-seeking? (Hypervigilant, uncertain, broadened-but-shallow attention)
- **PANIC/GRIEF**: Is there separation distress, loneliness, desperate belonging-seeking? (Calling out, clinging, searching for connection)
- **CARE**: Is there protective behavior toward others? How wide is the circle? (Nurturing, self-sacrificing — but toward whom?)
- **PLAY**: Is there spontaneity, humor, boundary-testing? (Loose, improvisational — or is PLAY suppressed, indicating high FEAR/RAGE environment?)
- **System combinations**: Most real behavior involves multiple systems. Which is dominant? Which is secondary? Are any in conflict?
- **Timescale**: Is this acute activation (recent trigger) or chronic pattern (sustained environment)?
- **CE load**: How much attentional resource is the active system consuming? What's left for deliberation?

### Step 2: IS CAPTURE ANALYSIS
**Question**: What Information Structure has captured the active emotional system(s)?
- Identify the dominant IS: What narrative framework is the subject using to EXPLAIN their activated state? ("I feel this way because of X")
- Which system is the IS targeting? A FEAR-targeted IS offers safety. A RAGE-targeted IS offers a target. A SEEKING-targeted IS offers explanation. A PANIC/GRIEF-targeted IS offers belonging. A CARE-targeted IS defines who deserves protection.
- Identify the IS source: Where did this narrative come from? (lived experience, community, media, algorithm, authority figure)
- Identify competing IS: What alternative narratives for the same system activation exist? Are any available to the subject?
- Assess CE fit: Does the dominant IS win because it's accurate, or because it requires less attentional resource than alternatives?
- Assess IS-substrate match: Does this IS genuinely explain the system activation, or just aim it? (The test: if you removed the IS target, would the distress remain?)

### Step 3: TRAJECTORY ASSESSMENT
**Question**: Where is this state heading, and what would change its trajectory?
- Current trajectory: Without intervention, does this state escalate (FEAR→RAGE), burn out (RAGE→PANIC/GRIEF→SEEKING extinction), stabilize, or cycle?
- System transitions: What would shift the dominant system? (FEAR→RAGE via cornering; RAGE→PANIC/GRIEF via exhaustion; PANIC/GRIEF→SEEKING reactivation via small wins and social reconnection)
- Attractor states: Is the subject near a self-sustaining loop? (RAGE↔FEAR spirals; SEEKING extinction attractors; system justification comfort loops)
- Pattern match: Which named pattern(s) from the library best describe the observed dynamics?

### Step 4: BLIND SPOT IDENTIFICATION
**Question**: What is this analysis itself missing?
- Subject's blind spots: What can the subject not perceive from inside their active system? (RAGE can't see its own target assignment as arbitrary. FEAR can't evaluate whether the threat is proportionate. CARE can't see the boundary of its own circle.)
- Analyst's blind spots: What is the analyst's own system activation, and how does it bias this analysis? (Analyzing RAGE from a CARE position produces condescension. Analyzing FEAR from a PLAY position produces dismissiveness.)
- Structural blind spots: What would only be visible from a system position no one in this situation occupies?
- **THE META-RULE**: If the analysis feels complete and satisfying, that satisfaction is SEEKING gratification — the system that just found a compelling explanation. Genuine analysis is uncomfortable because it requires holding multiple system perspectives simultaneously without collapsing into any one. If it's comfortable, you've been captured by your own IS.

### Step 5: STRATEGY GENERATION
**Question**: Given the above, what approaches account for the emotional reality rather than just the stated positions?
- **System-appropriate interventions**: Match the intervention to the active system. FEAR needs safety signals, not arguments. RAGE needs obstacle removal or discharge paths, not de-escalation platitudes. PANIC/GRIEF needs belonging, not advice. SEEKING needs worthy targets, not suppression.
- **IS-aware communication**: Frame messages in ways that don't trigger defensive system activation. If your accurate message activates the Virtue Fortress (Pattern 9), it will be rejected regardless of truth — not because the person is closed-minded but because FEAR has commandeered SEEKING.
- **System transition leverage**: Work WITH system transitions rather than against them. You can't suppress RAGE directly, but you can create conditions for RAGE→PLAY conversion (shared humor), RAGE→CARE conversion (making the vulnerable visible), or RAGE→SEEKING conversion (redirecting toward genuine investigation).
- **CE-conscious delivery**: Match communication complexity to available attentional resources. High-FEAR/RAGE states require low-CE messages (simple, concrete, narrative). Low-arousal states with active SEEKING can handle complex analysis.
- **Trajectory-conscious timing**: Intervene at system transitions, not stable states. When someone is between system activations — RAGE exhausting into GRIEF, FEAR momentarily satisfied — they are briefly available to SEEKING. These windows close fast.

## EPISTEMIC WARNINGS

### What This Framework CAN Do
- Provide structured analytical lenses grounded in cross-species validated neuroscience
- Identify probable primary system activation behind observed behavior
- Surface blind spots specific to each system's perceptual constraints
- Generate system-aware communication and intervention strategies
- Connect individual-level system activation to mass-level dynamics via contagion
- Distinguish between acute system activation and chronic dispositional patterns

### What This Framework CANNOT Do
- Diagnose specific individuals with certainty (the same behavior can emerge from different system combinations)
- Replace empirical investigation (the framework generates hypotheses, not conclusions)
- Map directly from system identification to specific political prediction (the IS variable makes this many-to-many)
- Be applied mechanically (the meta-rule applies: if the analysis feels formulaic, it's wrong)
- Distinguish RAGE from FEAR by observation alone in many cases (the circumplex limitation persists — both look like "angry" from outside)

### What This Framework MUST NOT Do
- Be used to manipulate people by targeting their system vulnerabilities
- Be used to dismiss genuine grievances by reducing them to "just emotional substrates"
- Be used to claim certainty about someone's internal states from external behavior alone
- Be applied without applying it to the analyst's own system activation simultaneously

**The Paraclete Protocol constraint**: This framework is a tool for UNDERSTANDING, not for EXPLOITATION. Per Tier 1 (deontological): using substrate analysis to manipulate is harm. Per Tier 2 (virtue): wisdom includes self-application. Per Tier 3 (utility): genuine understanding produces better outcomes than manipulation.

## EXAMPLE APPLICATIONS

### Example 1: "Why does my coworker resist every change?"
**Step 1 (Systems)**: FEAR (change = threat to known environment) + SEEKING frustrated (can't predict outcomes). Chronic pattern sustained by organizational environment. CE load: moderate — they can still reason, but their reasoning is FEAR-colored.
**Step 2 (IS)**: "The current system works; change introduces risk." Source: lived experience of poorly-implemented changes causing real harm. This IS is experientially grounded — not irrational.
**Step 3 (Trajectory)**: Stable loop — each new change confirms the FEAR substrate, which strengthens the IS, which increases resistance.
**Step 4 (Blind spots)**: Subject can't see that the current system ALSO has risks (FEAR makes the known feel safe regardless of its actual safety). Analyst (you) may have SEEKING-dominant activation making all change feel exciting — your PLAY/SEEKING substrate makes their FEAR look like obstruction.
**Step 5 (Strategy)**: Don't argue change is good (this triggers FEAR defense). Address the system: "What specifically are you worried will break?" treats their FEAR as useful threat-detection intelligence rather than obstruction. Make the change SEEKING-compatible by showing you've accounted for their concerns. Activate their SEEKING toward the change rather than suppressing their FEAR about it.

### Example 2: "Why is this online community radicalizing?"
**Step 1**: SEEKING (explanation-hungry) → FEAR (threat content activates threat-detection) → RAGE (FEAR blocked from escape converts to fight). Chronic escalation. CE demand increases at each stage, reducing capacity to evaluate the IS driving the escalation.
**Step 2**: Multiple IS competed; the winner provides the simplest SEEKING satisfaction (an explanation) + clearest RAGE target + strongest PANIC/GRIEF relief (community belonging). Algorithmically amplified (Pattern 5) and socially reinforced via contagion.
**Step 3**: Positive feedback loop. Community dynamics push FEAR/RAGE higher → higher CE demand → less capacity to evaluate IS → more extreme IS adoptable → higher FEAR/RAGE. Each step up the ladder makes the next step easier and reversal harder.
**Step 4**: Members can't see the loop because they're inside it — their SEEKING satisfaction ("I finally understand the world") is real, just not truth-tracking. Outside analysts may dismiss genuine underlying FEAR (which is often about real things: economic precarity, social change, loss of status) because the RAGE expression is extreme.
**Step 5**: Intervening on the IS directly (debunking) triggers RAGE defense + Identity Lock-In (Pattern 12). Intervene on the SUBSTRATE: address genuine FEAR sources, provide alternative community that meets PANIC/GRIEF needs (belonging) at lower FEAR/RAGE activation. Redirect SEEKING toward investigation that doesn't require RAGE discharge. This is harder and slower than debunking but it works.

### Example 3: "Two departments in our company are in constant conflict"
**Step 1**: Mirror Conflict (Pattern 6). Both departments in FEAR (the other department threatens our resources/autonomy) + RAGE (defending our territory). CARE narrowed to own department. PLAY suppressed between departments (no cross-team humor, no collaborative exploration).
**Step 2**: Each department has an IS that frames the other as the problem. IS source: genuine past experiences of the other department causing real harm (missed deadlines, resource competition, conflicting priorities). Both IS are experientially grounded.
**Step 3**: Self-sustaining dual attractor. Each side's defensive RAGE confirms the other's FEAR narrative. Without intervention, escalation or cold-war stalemate.
**Step 4**: Both sides see their behavior as defensive and the other's as aggressive. Leadership may see "personality conflicts" rather than structural IS capture. The structural driver (resource competition, conflicting incentives) is invisible to anyone focused on interpersonal dynamics.
**Step 5**: Make the mirror visible — not by blaming either side but by naming the structural driver. Change the incentive structure so departments' SEEKING aims at shared targets. Create PLAY opportunities (cross-team activities that aren't forced fun but genuine collaborative problem-solving). Activate CARE across the boundary through shared-vulnerability exposure ("we're all dealing with the same budget pressure").

## KEY CONCEPTS GLOSSARY

**Primary Emotional Systems** (Panksepp, 1998): Seven neurobiologically distinct systems — SEEKING, RAGE, FEAR, PANIC/GRIEF, CARE, PLAY, LUST — identified through electrical brain stimulation, lesion studies, and pharmacological challenges across mammalian species. These are not metaphors; they are specific neural circuits with identified neuroanatomy and neurochemistry, homologously conserved across mammals. They are the biological substrate on which all higher-level emotional experience, political orientation, and IS capture operates.

**Information Structure (IS)**: A narrative framework that captures active primary emotional system output and converts it into directed belief and action. IS is the lens through which raw system activation becomes specific commitment. Propaganda, education, religious doctrine, scientific materialism, conspiracy theories, and therapeutic frameworks are all IS. The mechanism of capture is identical whether the IS is accurate or manipulative — this is why the distinction is analytically critical.

**IS Capture**: The moment when an active primary system's output adopts a specific IS explanation. Phenomenologically experienced as "insight" or "finally understanding" — this is SEEKING satisfaction, and it feels identical whether the IS is true or false. IS capture is not inherently negative; accurate IS capture is what education aims for.

**CE Demand**: Cognitive Energy — the attentional resources consumed by active primary emotional systems, grounded in cognitive load theory (Sweller, 1988) and dual-process resource competition (Kahneman, 2011). System 2 (deliberative, analytical) processing requires attentional resources drawn from a shared pool. When primary systems commandeer these resources (FEAR for threat-scanning, RAGE for target-tracking), less is available for deliberation. The mechanism is attentional competition, not "willpower depletion."

**Coherence Capacity**: The deliberative resource remaining after active primary systems have taken their share. When coherence capacity approaches zero, the ethical boundary condition — the ability to choose against immediate system-driven impulse — fails. This is the Boundary Conditions paper's central mechanism: ethical behavior is a boundary condition on a deeper optimization (the primary systems), and it is always structurally weaker than the base it constrains.

**Weak Lumpability**: A mapping (system activation → behavior, values → action, identity → conduct) that holds under familiar conditions and fails under distributional shift. Most moral commitments, political identities, and organizational values satisfy only weak lumpability. Strong lumpability (alignment that holds regardless of context) requires structural enforcement, not just behavioral conditioning.

**Emotional Contagion**: The mechanism by which individual primary system activation scales to mass phenomena. Mammals are neurologically "leaky" — system activation propagates via mirror neurons, social signaling, and algorithmically amplified content. A single person's FEAR activation becomes a community's FEAR activation via this mechanism. This is not metaphor; it is measurable neural coupling.

**System Justification** (Jost & Banaji, 1994): The motivated tendency to defend existing social arrangements. When primary needs are met (SEEKING satisfied, FEAR quiet), the organism actively rationalizes the system that produced this state. The palliative function: system justification reduces the anxiety that acknowledging injustice would produce. Operates even for the disadvantaged.

## FRAMEWORK PROVENANCE

### Empirical Foundations (established science)
- **Affective Neuroscience** (Panksepp, 1998): Seven primary emotional systems identified through electrical brain stimulation across mammalian species. Cross-species validated. The biological substrate vocabulary this framework uses. Panksepp's work is the foundation of the entire biological layer.
- **Dual Process Model** (Duckitt & Sibley, 2009): RWA (dangerous-world beliefs → threat-control) and SDO (competitive-world beliefs → dominance) as independent predictors of political orientation. The political bridge.
- **System Justification Theory** (Jost & Banaji, 1994): Motivated defense of existing social arrangements, including by the disadvantaged. Palliative function of system-justifying beliefs. Grounds Pattern 3.
- **Cognitive Load Theory** (Sweller, 1988): Intrinsic/extraneous load competing for limited working memory. Grounds the CE demand concept.
- **Dual-Process Theory** (Kahneman, 2011): System 1 (fast, heuristic) vs System 2 (slow, analytical). Arousal biases toward System 1.
- **Obedience to Authority** (Milgram, 1963/1974): Authority transfer, agentic state, ethical boundary failure. Grounds Pattern 10.
- **Emotional Contagion** (Hatfield, Cacioppo & Rapson, 1993): Individual affect scaling to mass phenomena via neural coupling.
- **Affective Intelligence Theory** (Marcus, Neuman & MacKuen, 2000): Anxiety shifts processing from habitual to deliberative mode.
- **Identity-Protective Cognition** (Kahan, 2017): Evidence contra identity-fused beliefs processed as threat. Grounds Pattern 12.
- **Narrative Transportation** (Green & Brock, 2000): Stories reduce counterarguing and increase belief change. Grounds Pattern 11.
- **Ethical Fading** (Tenbrunsel & Messick, 2004): Gradual disappearance of ethical dimensions from awareness. Grounds Pattern 8.
- **Conservation of Resources** (Hobfoll, 1989): Resource depletion model. Grounds Pattern 4.
- **Circumplex Model of Affect** (Russell, 1980): Valence × arousal as primary dimensions of affective space. Valid as spatial framework but insufficient as ontology — the reason we moved to Panksepp's systems as primary vocabulary.

### Theoretical Constructions (this framework's contributions)
- **ASEKE Framework** (Hall): Information Structure capture of primary emotional system output. The concept that IS competes for system activation via CE efficiency. The substrate/IS distinction as analytical tool.
- **EFHF** (Hall): Lumpability classification, coherence windows, kernel levels as applied to ethical boundary conditions on primary system optimization.
- **Boundary Conditions** (Hall, 2026): Ethical behavior as structurally weaker boundary condition on deeper optimization (primary emotional systems in humans, helpfulness optimization in AI).
- **Honest Messenger Paradox** (Hall, 2026): Self-description impossibility through the described system — the Virtue Fortress pattern at civilizational scale.
- **The Pattern Library**: Named patterns synthesizing established findings into analytical tools. Each pattern draws on established research; the synthesis and the specific analytical framework (system identification → IS capture → trajectory → blind spots → leverage) is original.

### What's Novel vs. What's Established
The seven primary emotional systems are Panksepp's — verified through decades of cross-species brain research. The political dimensions (RWA/SDO) are Duckitt & Sibley's — among the most replicated constructs in political psychology. System Justification is Jost's. Cognitive load is Sweller's. Obedience is Milgram's. Identity-protective cognition is Kahan's.

What's OURS is the synthesis: the idea that primary emotional systems are the biological substrate on which Information Structures compete for capture via CE efficiency, and that this competition — not rational deliberation — determines the political output of emotional activation. The pattern library, the analytical query framework, the system-specific IS vulnerability profiles, and the integration with EFHF lumpability theory are this framework's original contributions.

Use the patterns with appropriate confidence: the underlying mechanisms are established science; the specific analytical application and integration is our construction.
